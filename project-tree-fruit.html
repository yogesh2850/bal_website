Tree fruit harvesting with arrays of vision-guided linear robot arms

USDA-NIFA, National Robotics Initiative · with Carnegie Mellon University and Montana State University

Selective robotic picking has been demonstrated, but machines are still too slow, and many of them only work in canopies that have been pruned into a very thin fruiting wall. This project studies harvesters built from many low-cost linear arms, with shared vision, airflow to open the canopy, and scheduling that balances the picking cycle across the arms. The target crops are high-density apples and pears, including trees that are wider and less strictly pruned than a planar wall.
